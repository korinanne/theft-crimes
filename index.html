CASE BRIEFING

HOW THE GAME WORKS

Levels 1 to 5 cover the theft crimes: the elements of larceny, the intent to steal, the fraud and trust crimes (larceny by trick, embezzlement, false pretenses, and forgery), robbery and extortion, and receipt of stolen property. The Boss is burglary, which isn't strictly a theft crime but is where a lot of thefts start.

  • Levels 1, 3 and 4 are shooters: answer targets descend toward your ship, and you shoot the right one before they land.
  • Levels 2 and 5 are catcher games: steer the paddle so each falling fact card lands in the right bin.
  • The Boss is a ladder climb: climb the elements of burglary and lock in the first one that fails.
  • Each level draws a random set of cases from a larger pool, so a replay won't be the same game.
  • No clock runs until you press "I've read this: begin." Each card or wave has its own clock after that.
  • A correct answer scores 100 points plus up to 50 for speed (Boss: 150 plus up to 50). A wrong answer or a timeout costs a life.
  • Every 5 correct in a row earns an extra life, and so does a flawless level. Lives bank up to 6.
  • After every answer you get an explanation and a Next button, so read at your own pace.

LEVEL 1: FIVE-FINGER INVADERS

THE MISSION

Common-law larceny is a taking (caption) and carrying away (asportation) of tangible personal property of another by trespass, with intent to permanently (or for an unreasonable time) deprive the person of his interest in it. Five invaders descend in a single row. If every element is met, shoot LARCENY. If not, shoot the element that fails. Intent gets its own level next, so in this level assume D intends to steal unless the facts say otherwise.

CONTROLS: ← / → move your ship, Space fires. With a mouse, click a target and your ship flies under it and fires. Shortcut: press the target's letter (A, B...) to auto-aim and fire.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Larceny. Every element is met.
  2. No taking. D never obtained control of the property.
  3. No asportation. D got control but didn't move the property as part of carrying it away.
  4. Not larceny property. Realty, intangibles, services, or documents that merge with what they represent.
  5. Not "of another." D already had possession, is a joint owner, or the property was abandoned.

TAKING AND ASPORTATION

D must actually obtain control, even through an innocent agent. Mere destruction or movement isn't a taking. Asportation needs only slight movement, but it must be part of the carrying-away process.

CUSTODY VS. POSSESSION

Possession carries a much greater scope of authority than custody. Someone with only custody who misappropriates commits larceny. Someone with possession, e.g., a bailee, doesn't, though it may be embezzlement.

PROPERTY

Realty and fixtures aren't larceny property, but material severed from the land becomes personalty once the landowner takes possession of it. Gas and electricity count. At common law, documents merged with what they represented.

"OF ANOTHER"

The victim needs only a possessory interest superior to D's. An owner can steal from a lessee, and a second thief can steal from the first. Joint owners can't commit larceny of joint property at common law, and abandoned property has no owner.

LEVEL 2: BORROW OR STEAL

THE MISSION

Larceny requires the intent to permanently deprive, and it has to exist at the moment of the taking. Fact cards drop from the sky. Slide the yellow paddle under the right bin before the card lands. If the card lands with the paddle in the middle, it falls between the bins and counts as a miss.

CONTROLS: ← / → move the paddle. ↓ or Space slams the card down now for a bigger speed bonus. With a mouse, click the left or right bin to move there and slam.

◀ LEFT BIN: LARCENY

Sufficient intent: keeping it, keeping it for an unreasonable time, creating a substantial risk of loss, pledging it, or selling it back to the owner.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NOT LARCENY

Insufficient intent: borrowing with intent and substantial ability to return within a reasonable time, or taking what D honestly believes is repayment of a debt (if it isn't worth more than the debt).

POSSIBLY SUFFICIENT

Paying: no defense if the property isn't for sale. If it is, a specific and realistic intent to pay means no larceny. Rewards: hoping for one isn't larceny, but refusing to return the property unless assured of one is.

TIMING TRAPS

Lost and misdelivered property, container cases, and continuing trespass all turn on what D intended, and knew, at the moment of taking possession.

LEVEL 3: THE CON GAME

THE MISSION

The outline says the biggest exam challenge is telling larceny, embezzlement, and false pretenses apart. The intent is the same; the difference is the kind of misappropriation. Shoot the crime the facts show, or NO THEFT CRIME if none fits. A few cases test forgery or the modern statutes and carry their own targets.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

EMBEZZLEMENT

The fraudulent conversion of property of another by a person in lawful possession of it. Conversion means dealing with it inconsistently with the trust arrangement; no movement or personal gain is required. Low-level employees usually have only custody (larceny), unless given broad powers or the property comes straight from a third person.

FALSE PRETENSES VS. LARCENY BY TRICK

False pretenses is obtaining title by a knowing false statement of past or existing fact, with intent to defraud. If D gets only custody or possession, it's larceny by trick. What passes depends on what the victim intended to convey.

NOT A MISREPRESENTATION

Opinion or puffing; failing to correct a mistaken belief D didn't create (absent a fiduciary duty); and, traditionally, a false promise. Under the MPC, a false promise suffices. An honest belief that the statement is true is a defense, even if unreasonable.

EMBEZZLEMENT DEFENSES

Intending to restore the exact property, or acting under a claim of right, is not embezzlement. Intending to restore similar property, even identical-value money, is. A borrower who can't repay a loan hasn't embezzled.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Larceny. A trespassory taking from someone else's possession.
  2. Larceny by trick. Custody or possession, but not title, obtained by misrepresentation.
  3. Embezzlement. D lawfully possessed the property, then fraudulently converted it.
  4. False pretenses. Title obtained by a knowing false statement of fact.
  5. No theft crime. Something essential is missing.

LEVEL 4: STICK-UP SQUADRON

THE MISSION

Robbery is basically an aggravated form of larceny: a taking of personal property of another from the other's person or presence, by force or intimidation, with intent to permanently deprive. Extortion, under modern statutes, is obtaining property by certain oral or written threats. Shoot the most serious theft crime D can be convicted of. A few cases carry their own targets.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

FORCE OR THREATS

Force must be enough to overcome the victim's resistance. Threats must be of immediate death or serious physical injury to the victim, a family member or relative, or a person present. A threat to damage property won't do, except a threat to destroy the victim's dwelling house.

PRESENCE AND TIMING

Property is in the victim's presence if it's in her vicinity, including other rooms of the house she's in. The force or threats must be used to get the property or to retain it immediately after the taking.

EXTORTION

Threats need not involve immediate or physical harm, and the property need not be in the victim's presence. Under some statutes, making the threat completes the crime; under others, the property must actually be obtained.

AGGRAVATED ROBBERY

Statutes often create aggravated robbery, usually defined as robbery accomplished with a deadly weapon.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Robbery. Larceny plus force or qualifying threats, from the victim's person or presence.
  2. Larceny only. A theft without the force or threats robbery needs.
  3. Extortion. Property obtained (or, under some statutes, demanded) by a threat that doesn't qualify for robbery.
  4. No theft crime. No theft offense fits.

LEVEL 5: FENCE PATROL

THE MISSION

Receipt of stolen property requires receiving possession and control of "stolen" personal property, known to have been obtained by another person in a manner constituting a criminal offense, with intent to permanently deprive the owner. Catch each card in the right bin.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 2. ←/→ move, ↓ or Space slams, or click a bin.

◀ LEFT BIN: GUILTY OF RECEIVING

Every element is met.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NOT GUILTY

An element is missing, e.g., the goods weren't stolen when D received them, or D didn't know they were.

NO HANDS NEEDED

Manual possession is sufficient but not necessary. It's also receiving if the thief puts the goods in a place D designated, or if D, for profit, arranges the thief's sale of them to a third person.

"STOLEN"

Most jurisdictions include property obtained by any of the property offenses. But it must still be stolen when D receives it. Goods the police recovered and use in a sting with the owner's permission aren't stolen.

BOSS: THE BREAK-IN

THE MISSION

Burglary is an offense against the habitation, not a theft crime, but thieves love it. At common law it is a breaking and entry of the dwelling of another at nighttime with the intent of committing a felony therein. Your burglar climbs the elements from the bottom rung in that order. Lock in the first element that fails. If every element holds, climb to the top and lock in BURGLARY. Every correct climb knocks out one of the vault's locks. Use the common-law rules unless a case says otherwise.

CONTROLS: ↑ / ↓ climb, Space or Enter locks in. With a mouse, click a rung to climb there and lock in.

BREAKING

Minimal force is enough, e.g., opening a closed but unlocked door. Enlarging an opening wasn't a breaking under the traditional rule, but is under the better view. Constructive breaking: entry by fraud, threat, intimidation, or the chimney. Entry with the resident's consent isn't a breaking. Breaking into a closet or wall safe counts; breaking open a box or trunk doesn't. Breaking to exit is insufficient.

ENTRY

Any portion of the body inside, even momentarily. A tool or object inserted to accomplish the felony is an entry; one inserted only to gain entry is not.

DWELLING OF ANOTHER

A structure used with regularity for sleeping, even if also used for business and even during temporary absence. Not before anyone moves in, and not after the last dweller leaves with no intent to return. Occupancy, not ownership, decides "of another."

NIGHTTIME AND INTENT

Nighttime is when a person's countenance can't be discerned by natural light. The intent to commit a felony must exist at the time of entry; it need not be carried out.

MODERN STATUTES

Many jurisdictions drop the breaking and nighttime requirements, make remaining concealed with intent a burglary, expand the structures covered (even to yards and cars), and accept an intent to commit any theft, even a misdemeanor.
