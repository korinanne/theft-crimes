CASE BRIEFING

HOW THE GAME WORKS

Levels 1 to 5 cover the theft crimes: the elements of larceny, the intent to steal, the fraud and trust crimes (larceny by trick, embezzlement, false pretenses, and forgery), robbery and extortion, and receiving stolen property. The Boss is burglary, which isn't strictly a theft crime but is where a lot of thefts start.

  • Levels 1, 3 and 4 are shooters: answer targets descend toward your ship, and you shoot the right one before they land.
  • Levels 2 and 5 are catcher games: steer the paddle so each falling fact card lands in the right bin.
  • The Boss is a ladder climb: climb the elements of burglary and lock in the first one that fails.
  • Each level draws a random set of cases from a larger pool, so a replay won't be the same game.
  • No clock runs until you press "I've read this: begin." Each card or wave has its own clock after that.
  • A correct answer scores 100 points plus up to 50 for speed (Boss: 150 plus up to 50). A wrong answer or a timeout costs a life.
  • Every 5 correct in a row earns an extra life, and so does a flawless level. Lives bank up to 6.
  • After every answer you get an explanation and a Next button, so read at your own pace.

LEVEL 1: FIVE-FINGER INVADERS

THE MISSION

Common-law larceny is the trespassory taking and carrying away of the personal property of another with the intent to permanently deprive that person of it. Five invaders descend in a single row. If every element is met, shoot LARCENY. If not, shoot the element that fails. Intent gets its own level next, so in this level assume D intends to keep the property unless the facts say otherwise.

CONTROLS: ← / → move your ship, Space fires. With a mouse, click a target and your ship flies under it and fires. Shortcut: press the target's letter (A, B...) to auto-aim and fire.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Larceny. Every element is met.
  2. No trespass. The owner consented to D having the property, so the original taking wasn't trespassory.
  3. No taking or carrying away. Nothing moved from the owner's possession into D's control (caption and asportation).
  4. Not personal property. Real property, services, or intangibles that don't count.
  5. Not another's property. D has an equal right to possess it, or no one has rights in it anymore.

CAPTION AND ASPORTATION

Taking (caption) is any trespassory removal from the owner's possession into another's control, including through an agent. Carrying away (asportation) needs only slight movement, e.g., inches.

CONTINUING TRESPASS

If the original taking was wrongful but D didn't yet intend to steal (e.g., taking to borrow), the trespass continues, and larceny occurs when D later forms the intent to steal.

LEVEL 2: BORROW OR STEAL

THE MISSION

Larceny is a specific-intent crime: the intent to permanently deprive must exist at the time of the taking. Fact cards drop from the sky. Slide the yellow paddle under the right bin before the card lands. If the card lands with the paddle in the middle, it falls between the bins and counts as a miss.

CONTROLS: ← / → move the paddle. ↓ or Space slams the card down now for a bigger speed bonus. With a mouse, click the left or right bin to move there and slam.

◀ LEFT BIN: INTENT TO STEAL

Intending to keep it, abandon it, sell it back to the owner, pawn it with no ability to redeem it, return it only upon a reward, or create a substantial risk of its loss.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NO INTENT TO STEAL

Intending to borrow it with the ability to return it, to pay for merchandise D has the means to buy, or to take money as repayment of a debt under an honest claim of right.

TIMING AND CHANGE OF HEART

The crime is complete at the taking. Restoring the property after a change of heart is no defense. A mistake of fact negates the intent to steal even if the mistake was unreasonable.

LEVEL 3: THE CON GAME

THE MISSION

Larceny, larceny by trick, embezzlement, and false pretenses all end with D holding V's property. What separates them is how D got it and what passed. Shoot the crime the facts show, or NO THEFT CRIME if none fits. A few cases test forgery or the modern theft statutes and carry their own targets.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

LARCENY VS. EMBEZZLEMENT

Embezzlement is the fraudulent conversion of another's property by someone in lawful possession of it. Larceny requires a trespassory taking. A low-level employee has only custody (larceny); a high-level employee may have possession (embezzlement).

LARCENY BY TRICK VS. FALSE PRETENSES

Both require a false representation of a material past or present fact that the victim relies on. Larceny by trick gets D possession. False pretenses gets D title.

NOT A REPRESENTATION

A prediction, a false promise, or an opinion such as sales talk or puffing. Silence isn't one either, unless D caused the misunderstanding or owed V a fiduciary duty.

BAILEES

A bailee has possession and can embezzle. But a bailee who opens the bailor's closed container and takes the contents with intent to steal commits larceny.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Larceny. A trespassory taking, with no lawful possession.
  2. Larceny by trick. V hands over possession only, induced by fraud.
  3. Embezzlement. D lawfully possessed the property, then fraudulently converted it.
  4. False pretenses. V passes title in reliance on D's known false statement of fact.
  5. No theft crime. Something essential is missing.

LEVEL 4: STICK-UP SQUADRON

THE MISSION

Robbery is larceny from the person or presence of the victim by force or intimidation. Extortion is taking money or property by threat, under the modern statutes. Shoot the most serious theft crime D can be convicted of.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 1. ←/→ + Space, click a target, or press its letter.

FORCE

Slight force is enough, but it must be more than what's needed to take and carry away the property. An unnoticed pickpocketing isn't robbery. Neither is a purse-snatching in most states, unless the victim notices and resists or is knocked down, struck, or injured.

INTIMIDATION

A threat of immediate serious physical injury to the victim, a close family member, or another person present. A threat to damage property, other than the victim's home, generally isn't enough.

TIMING

The common law requires the force before or at the same time as the taking. The modern trend accepts force immediately after it (e.g., during escape). Property is in the victim's presence if it's within reach or control.

EXTORTION VS. ROBBERY

Extortion's threats need not be of immediate or physical harm, and the property need not be on the victim or in the victim's presence. In most jurisdictions, making the threat is the crime. Larceny, assault, and battery merge into robbery.

READ THE TARGETS FIRST

  1. Robbery. Larceny plus force or intimidation, from the victim's person or presence.
  2. Larceny only. A theft without the force or intimidation robbery needs.
  3. Extortion. Obtaining (or, in most places, threatening to obtain) property by a threat that doesn't qualify as robbery.
  4. No theft crime. No theft offense fits.

LEVEL 5: FENCE PATROL

THE MISSION

Receiving stolen property is a statutory crime: receiving control of stolen property, knowing it is stolen, with intent to permanently deprive the owner. Catch each card in the right bin. Unless a card says otherwise, assume the traditional rule: D must actually know the property is stolen, and that knowledge must exist when D receives it.

CONTROLS: Same as Level 2. ←/→ move, ↓ or Space slams, or click a bin.

◀ LEFT BIN: GUILTY OF RECEIVING

The property was stolen when received (e.g., through larceny, embezzlement, or false pretenses), D had control of it, D knew it was stolen at the time, and D intended to permanently deprive the owner.

RIGHT BIN ▶: NOT GUILTY

An element is missing, e.g., D learned the truth only later, or D never intended to deprive the owner.

CONTROL IS ENOUGH

Only control, not possession, is necessary.

OTHER APPROACHES

Modern view: knowledge can be inferred from facts that would alert a reasonable person. "Possession of stolen property" statutes don't require the knowledge to coincide with receipt.

BOSS: THE BREAK-IN

THE MISSION

Burglary isn't strictly a theft crime, but thieves love it. Common-law burglary is the breaking and entering of the dwelling of another at nighttime with the specific intent to commit a felony inside. Your burglar climbs the elements from the bottom rung in that order. Lock in the first element that fails. If every element holds, climb to the top and lock in BURGLARY. Every correct climb knocks out one of the vault's locks. Use the common-law rules unless a case says otherwise.

CONTROLS: ↑ / ↓ climb, Space or Enter locks in. With a mouse, click a rung to climb there and lock in.

BREAKING

Using force, even slight force (e.g., opening an unlocked door), to create an opening. Walking through an open door isn't a breaking unless the opening must be enlarged. Entry by fraud or threat is a breaking. With consent, breaking into part of the structure (a closet door or wall safe) counts, but opening a desk drawer, trunk, or box doesn't. Force used to exit doesn't count.

ENTERING

Any part of D's body (e.g., a hand through a broken window), or an instrument used to gain entry, crosses into the dwelling through the opening. Breaking and entering need not happen at the same time.

DWELLING OF ANOTHER

A structure regularly occupied for habitation. It need not be occupied at the time, but it can't be abandoned. You can't burglarize your own dwelling, but an owner who transferred possession (e.g., to a tenant) can.

NIGHTTIME AND INTENT

Nighttime is darkness between sunset and sunrise, and it isn't nighttime if there's enough natural light to see the burglar's face. The intent to commit a felony must exist at the time of the breaking and entering.

MODERN STATUTES

Most states require only an entry, extend burglary to nondwellings (e.g., businesses and cars), drop the nighttime requirement (a few keep it), and include intended misdemeanor thefts. A completed felony inside doesn't merge with the burglary.
